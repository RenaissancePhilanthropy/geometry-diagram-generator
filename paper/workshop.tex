\begin{abstract}
Existing geometry benchmarks ask models to \emph{interpret} a diagram; none ask whether a model can \emph{produce} a correct diagram from a description.
We introduce \textbf{\GeoGen}, a methodology for automatically-verifiable benchmarks of generative geometric reasoning, and \textbf{\GeoGenBench}, its first instance: 801 prompts paired with machine-checkable property lists.
\GeoGen co-generates prompts and rubrics from shared Python source, then renders the model's TikZ output and decides each predicate symbolically against compiled SymPy coordinates --- no human grading, no vision--language judge.
Benchmarking six frontier models under a raw-TikZ and a structured-IR strategy, we find the cost--quality Pareto frontier collapses to a single corner: three flagships tie within $1$\pp at a $7\times$ cost spread under the IR, which lifts models by $2$--$25$\pp (most for those weakest at raw TikZ).
One vendor's model rejects the IR schema outright --- a portability gap, not a capability gap.
A pre-registered $N{=}200$ human study finds moderate auto/expert agreement ($\kappa{=}0.455$) with strictly one-directional verifier lenience.
We release the dataset, template engine, verification toolkit, and leaderboard.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Frontier LLMs now author visual artifacts --- slide decks, worksheets, technical illustrations.
The most-deployed such artifact in K--12 education is the geometry diagram, yet every existing geometry benchmark evaluates the inverse direction: given a diagram, answer a question.
None measures whether a model can \emph{produce} a correct diagram from a textual description.
\GeoGenBench fills this gap.

Consider \textit{``Draw an acute triangle $EFG$ with vertex angles $60^\circ$, $70^\circ$ at $E$ and $F$. Construct the altitude from $G$ meeting $EF$ at $H$.''}
A correct rendering must satisfy $\angle E = 60^\circ$, $\angle F = 70^\circ$, $H$ on segment $EF$ (not its extension), $\angle GHE = 90^\circ$, and acuteness.
\GeoGenBench encodes each clause as a machine-checkable predicate over the SymPy-compiled coordinates of the model's rendered TikZ: in our pilot, one flagship placed $H$ on line $EF$ but \emph{outside} the segment; another drew an obtuse triangle.
The verifier flags each failure with a specific predicate verdict, not a holistic score.

We distinguish \textbf{\GeoGen}, the methodology --- (i) procedural co-generation of prompts and property-list rubrics from shared source, and (ii) a render-then-symbolic-verify pipeline driven by a declarative predicate language --- from \textbf{\GeoGenBench}, its 801-prompt plane-geometry instance.
Future \GeoGen-family benchmarks (coordinate-plane algebra, 3D solids, engineering drawings) inherit the methodology and extend the per-domain primitives.

\paragraph{Related work.}
Geometry3K \citep{lu2021intergps}, GeoQA \citep{chen2021geoqa}, UniGeo \citep{chen2022unigeo}, MathVista \citep{lu2024mathvista}, GeoEval \citep{zhang2024geoeval}, and GeoBench \citep{geobench2026} all measure diagram \emph{interpretation}.
On the generation side, T2I-CompBench++ \citep{huang2025t2icompbenchpp} and GenExam \citep{wang2025genexam} grade generated \emph{images} via CLIP/MLLM or LLM judges, which cannot tell whether a ``right triangle'' actually has a right angle --- only whether the image looks like one.
\GeoGenBench grades in symbolic space (TikZ $\to$ compiled SymPy geometry), where a \propname{right\_angle} verdict is an exact numeric check, deterministic, reproducible, and $\sim$$1000\times$ cheaper than an MLLM judge.
Lean/Isabelle corpora and AlphaGeometry \citep{trinh2024alphageometry} machine-check \emph{proofs}; \GeoGenBench extends automatic verification to \emph{diagram generation}, occupying the previously-empty generation-side, automatically-graded cell.

\section{The \GeoGen methodology and the \GeoGenBench instance}
\label{sec:design}

\paragraph{Pillar 1: Procedural co-generation.}
Both the natural-language prompt and the machine-checkable property list are emitted by the same Python template (\cref{fig:cogen}): a single \texttt{altitude\_template($A,B,C,H$)} returns both the prose request and the predicate list \texttt{[right\_angle($A$,$H$,$B$), point\_on\_segment($H$,$B$,$C$)]} any correct rendering must satisfy.
This eliminates rubric/question drift and provides a contamination defence: reviewers can re-roll the engine with fresh seeds at the same difficulty distribution.
Where co-generation is impractical, the methodology supports hand- or LLM-extracted prompts with hand-authored rubrics, reported separately by source.

\paragraph{Pillar 2: Render-then-symbolic-verify.}
The model emits TikZ; a containerised LuaLaTeX renderer compiles it to SVG; an extractor compiles the same source to a coordinate-bearing SymPy representation; a fixed-vocabulary, typed predicate language is decided against that representation in microseconds per predicate.
The v1 language comprises 17 symbolic predicates (e.g.\ \propname{right\_angle}, \propname{midpoint}, \propname{collinear}, \propname{parallel}, \propname{tangent}, \propname{angle\_equal}, \propname{point\_on\_segment}) decided with numeric tolerance $\tau = 5\times10^{-3}$, plus 2 intentionally-weaker source-string checks (label/mark presence) contributing only to a \emph{loose} pass.
The language is declarative (authors specify \emph{what} must hold, not \emph{how} to check it), versioned, and renderer-agnostic.

\paragraph{Task and scoring.}
A scenario is $\mathcal{S} = \langle \text{prompt},\ \mathcal{P},\ \mathcal{L},\ \mathcal{C} \rangle$; the model sees only the prompt --- properties are the rubric, never the input.
Each output scores \texttt{pass} (all checks pass), \texttt{soft\_pass} (no failures, $\geq1$ check skipped), \texttt{fail}, or a generation/render failure.

\paragraph{Composition.}
801 prompts: 600 templated scenarios from 30 procedural templates, 200 per difficulty tier --- \tone\ (single shape), \ttwo\ (one auxiliary construction), \tthree\ (multi-step composition) --- plus 201 curriculum scenarios LLM-extracted against the topical scaffold of a state-adopted K--12 curriculum \citep{bluebonnet2024}, adding breadth at the cost of LLM-authored ground truth.
Metrics are decomposed by source; the templated half serves as the calibration anchor.

\section{Verification reliability}
\label{sec:verification}

\paragraph{Completeness and adversarial soundness.}
A property list is \emph{complete} if every diagram passing it is congruent (up to rigid motion and scale) to the intended construction.
Auditing all 30 templates yields $18/11/1$ tight/loose/skeletal ($63\%/35\%/2\%$ of scenarios): loose templates pin the named relations but leave continuous parameters free, matching educational practice.
For each of the 17 symbolic predicates we attempted adversarial TikZ that passes the predicate while contradicting the prompt; five predicates show soft spots, all addressable by hardening or template-side pairing, and we estimate $<5\%$ of strict passes are vulnerable in the wild because templates pair check types so single-check loopholes close.

\paragraph{Hardening.}
Three verifier changes (closed-form intersection solving, a pgfmath-style coordinate evaluator, the \propname{centroid} predicate) promoted $4.6\%$ of pilot records from \texttt{soft\_pass} to \texttt{pass} with zero demotions --- and shrank the apparent structured-vs-raw strategy effect by roughly half, showing that part of the ``strategy gap'' was verifier weakness on raw outputs, not model weakness.
All reported numbers are post-hardening.

\paragraph{Human-correlation study.}
A pre-registered study ($N{=}200$; stratified sample over the pilot; two domain-expert raters --- a PhD mathematician and a K--12 mathematics educator --- rating blind to the auto-verdict, then meeting to consensus) yields $\kappa = 0.455$ on the binary collapse (95\% CI $[0.304, 0.590]$; balanced accuracy $0.684$): \textbf{moderate agreement}, below the pre-registered $0.70$ threshold.
Inter-rater agreement is substantial ($\kappa = 0.704$), so the instrument is consistent; the gap is between raters and verifier.
Crucially, the disagreement is \emph{one-directional}: $18\%$ of strict-pass records overturn to expert \texttt{fail}, zero the reverse.
Per-tier, the disagreement concentrates entirely on \ttwo\ ($\kappa = 0.160$ vs $0.651$/$0.796$ on \tone/\tthree), where property lists check the named auxiliary construction but not prompt-specified numeric parameters --- the loose-completeness pattern made empirical ($89\%$ of disagreements code as ``verifier missed prompt-numeric constraint'').
Leaderboard rates are therefore conservative against the verifier-bias axis, and cell \emph{differences} are largely preserved since the lenience applies uniformly across cells on affected templates; the v2 fix (numeric predicates lifting affected \ttwo\ templates to tight) is committed follow-up.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:results}

\paragraph{Setup.}
Six production models across three vendors ($\sim$$20\times$ price spread): \opus, \sonnet, \haiku, \gpt, \geminipro, \geminiflash; temperature $1.0$; published April-2026 rates.
Two reference strategies: \textbf{\rawcode} (the model emits TikZ directly from the prompt plus a TikZ tutorial; zero scaffolding) and \textbf{\structured} (the model emits typed JSON for a Pydantic intermediate representation --- points, segments, intersections, circles, render ops, and \emph{check} declarations --- which code compiles to SymPy, then TikZ; declared checks run before render, so the strategy self-corrects with up to 3 retries).
The headline run covers all 12 (model $\times$ strategy) cells at 600 templated scenarios $\times$ 3 repeats; a subprocess-isolated runner with an OS-level kill timeout keeps per-cell coverage $\geq 91\%$ except where noted.

\subsection{Findings}
\label{ssec:findings}

\paragraph{Strategy choice helps unevenly.}
\structured\ lifts every model: \gpt\ $+2.2$\pp, \haiku\ $+4.8$, \sonnet\ $+12.2$, \opus\ $+21.7$, \geminipro\ $+24.7$.
The lift is largest for the models weakest at \rawcode\ and smallest for the strongest.

\paragraph{Frontier convergence.}
Under \structured, \gpt\ ($94.1\%$, \dollars{0.075}/scen), \sonnet\ ($93.4\%$, \dollars{0.088}), and \opus\ ($93.3\%$, \dollars{0.532}) span $0.8$\pp at a $\mathbf{7\times}$ cost ratio: the frontier-tier model carries no measurable quality premium under IR scaffolding, and the Pareto frontier collapses to the \gpt + \structured\ corner.
On \rawcode\ the spread is wide ($91.9\% \to 35.9\%$); \opus + \rawcode\ is the most-dominated cell, beaten by \gpt + \rawcode\ by $20$\pp at $4.5\times$ lower cost.
By tier, \tone\ is near-saturated for every Anthropic/OpenAI \structured\ cell (v2 needs harder \tone\ templates); \ttwo\ is where strategy first matters (\opus\ $+43$\pp, \geminipro\ $+54$\pp); \tthree\ separates the frontier cells (\gpt\ $93\%$, \sonnet\ $87\%$, \opus\ $82\%$).
Most wrong outputs are \emph{rendered diagrams with wrong geometry}, not broken LaTeX.

\paragraph{Portability: the schema-state cap.}
\structured\ depends on schema-constrained generation, which OpenAI and Google compile to a server-side FSM with vendor-specific caps.
Our IR schema ($\sim$20-variant discriminated union) compiles to thousands of states; \geminiflash\ rejects it at the API layer (HTTP 400: \emph{``too many states for serving''}) before generating a single token.
This is a portability gap, not a capability gap --- and a caution for any benchmark shipping a multi-variant discriminated-union IR.

\section{Limitations and conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\GeoGenBench\ measures whether a model can translate a textual specification of a 2D plane-geometry construction into a globally-consistent metric realisation; it does not test proof construction, 3D solids, or teaching utility.
$35\%$ of templated scenarios are intentionally loose (a pass means \emph{a} diagram consistent with the stated constraints, not \emph{the} canonical one), and the human study localises the resulting verifier lenience to \ttwo\ numeric constraints, with the v2 fix identified.
On the cells run at headline N, the curriculum split is materially harder for every model measured (e.g.\ \haiku + \rawcode\ drops $82.1\% \to 36.0\%$); headline findings are templated-split findings.

\GeoGen's two pillars extend beyond plane geometry: co-generation reuses wherever a domain admits a Python-expressible construction, and render-then-symbolic-verify extends along the versioned predicate-language axis.
In flight: a GenExam-Math integration \citep{wang2025genexam} comparing symbolic verification against vision-LM judging on a shared 91-prompt subset, and coordinate-plane/3D extensions.
We release the dataset, template engine, verification toolkit, Docker renderer, and leaderboard data under permissive licenses.
