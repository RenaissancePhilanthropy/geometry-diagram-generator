\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Modern frontier LLMs author entire visual artifacts: slide decks, worksheets, technical illustrations, engineering diagrams.
The most-deployed visual artifact in the K--12 educational market is the geometry diagram --- a 2D plane figure paired with a problem statement.
Yet every existing geometry benchmark we are aware of evaluates the inverse direction: given a diagram, answer a question about it.
None measures whether a model can \emph{produce} a correct diagram from a textual description.
This is the gap \GeoGenBench fills.

\paragraph{Motivating example.}
\textit{``Draw an acute triangle $EFG$ with vertex angles $60^\circ$, $70^\circ$ at $E$ and $F$. Construct the altitude from $G$ meeting $EF$ at $H$.''}
A correct rendering satisfies: $\angle E = 60^\circ$, $\angle F = 70^\circ$, $H$ on segment $EF$ (not its extension), $\angle GHE = 90^\circ$, triangle acute.
\GeoGenBench encodes each clause as a machine-checkable predicate against the SymPy-compiled coordinates of the model's rendered TikZ output: in our pilot, \haiku\ and \opus\ pass under both strategies; \gpt + \rawcode\ places $H$ on the line $EF$ but \emph{outside} the segment; \sonnet + \rawcode\ draws an obtuse triangle.
The verifier flags each failure with a specific predicate verdict, not a holistic score (\cref{fig:cogen} illustrates the prompt$\to$rubric pipeline; the rendered-then-verified pipeline is in \cref{ssec:ver_defn}).

We use \textbf{\GeoGen} for the methodology and \textbf{\GeoGenBench} for the 801-prompt plane-geometry instance; the distinction matters because future instances (coordinate-plane algebra, 3D solids, engineering drawings) inherit the methodology while extending the per-domain primitives.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The \GeoGen methodology and verification toolkit.} Procedural co-generation that eliminates prompt/rubric drift; render-then-symbolic-verify driven by a versioned 17-predicate declarative language; a hardening audit (\cref{ssec:ver_defn}) closing $4.6\%$ of silently-skipped pilot checks. We release the engine, verifier, predicate language v1, and Docker renderer.
\item \textbf{The \GeoGenBench instance.} 801 prompts (600 procedurally co-generated, 201 LLM-extracted from a curriculum scaffold), six frontier models $\times$ two strategies on a $600$-scenario $\times$ $3$-repeat draw. Headline findings: a $58$\pp spread across eleven cells ($35.9\% \to 94.1\%$); a $7\times$ cost spread at tied quality between the three frontier+\structured cells; and a portability finding (\geminiflash's schema-state cap blocks structured generation; \cref{ssec:gemini_finding}). A pre-registered $N{=}200$ human-correlation study (\cref{ssec:human}) finds moderate auto/human agreement ($\kappa{=}0.455$) with one-directional verifier lenience on loose-completeness templates.
\end{enumerate}
