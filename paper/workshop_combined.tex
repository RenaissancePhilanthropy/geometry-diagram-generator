\begin{abstract}
Language models often answer math problems incorrectly while reporting high confidence.
We ask whether this reflects absent self-knowledge or a failure to express it, and separate the two by reading the residual stream directly --- anchored by a geometry-construction testbed whose outputs are graded exactly by a symbolic compiler, no human or model judge.
Across four open models spanning distinct architectures and four domains (compiler-verified geometry, MMLU-Pro, MATH, GPQA-Diamond), a linear probe on the residual stream predicts an attempt's correctness better than the model's stated confidence in 15 of 16 cells (mean $+0.11$ AUROC), with the largest gap on MATH, where wrong answers carry no surface tell.
The signal is attempt-specific (it survives a within-question control that collapses the model's own P(True) toward chance), largely \emph{domain-general} (probes transfer across domains at $\approx$$87$--$90\%$ AUROC retention), and \emph{causally used}: amplifying the model's own correctness direction, with no labels at intervention time, drops stated confidence on wrong answers from 80 to 43 while correct answers hold near 90, halving calibration error.
Models substantially know when they are wrong; whether that knowledge reaches speech varies by architecture, and we outline a global-workspace test of why.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A mathematical agent that errs is unavoidable; one that errs \emph{and knows it} can abstain, retry, or escalate.
Stated confidence is a weak guide to correctness, and weakest exactly where mathematics is hardest: a wrong multi-step derivation ends in a well-formed \texttt{\textbackslash boxed\{answer\}} with no surface tell.
We decompose the problem into two questions: do models internally represent that an output is wrong (\textbf{knowing}), and does that representation reach what they report (\textbf{saying})?
The gap between them, if it exists, is both an unused reliability signal and a concrete claim about machine introspection.

Prior work shows output probabilities are often better calibrated than words \citep{kadavath2022language}, and that the truth of \emph{given statements} is linearly readable from activations \citep{azaria2023internal, burns2023discovering, marks2024geometry}; verbalized-calibration studies measure what models say \citep{lin2022teaching, tian2023just}.
We combine the threads on the model's \emph{own attempts}: temporal (pre/post) confidence elicitation, blind (no-feedback) updating, four readouts of the same latent quantity on identical records, and an intervention \citep{zou2023representation}.
Measuring this well requires ground-truth correctness labels on open-ended generation at scale --- which is why the study is anchored by a machine-verified testbed (\cref{sec:testbed}).
Our findings (\cref{sec:knowing}): the internal signal exceeds the spoken one almost everywhere, is specific to the individual attempt, transfers across domains, and causally sets stated confidence --- to an architecture-dependent degree that we argue is itself the finding.

\section{The instrument: ground truth without a judge}
\label{sec:testbed}

Metacognition experiments live or die by their correctness labels, and LLM-judged labels inherit the judge's blind spots.
Our anchor domain avoids judges entirely.
In \GeoGenBench, a model is asked in natural language for a geometric construction (``draw an acute triangle $EFG$ with $\angle E{=}60^\circ$, $\angle F{=}70^\circ$; construct the altitude from $G$ meeting $EF$ at $H$''); its output is compiled to SymPy coordinates, and each prompt clause is decided as an exact symbolic predicate (15 typed predicates, tolerance $5{\times}10^{-3}$) --- deterministic, reproducible, $\sim$$1000\times$ cheaper than a vision--language judge, and hard for current open models (13--39\% pass).
Prompts and rubrics are co-generated from shared Python source, so the grader can never drift from the question.
A pre-registered human study ($N{=}200$, two trained raters, blind) finds the verifier's disagreements with humans are strictly \emph{one-directional} (lenient, never harsh), so a \texttt{fail} label --- the label metacognition needs --- is trustworthy.
Benchmarking six frontier models on the 801-prompt suite is reported elsewhere; the two findings that matter here are that a structured intermediate representation lifts every model ($+2$ to $+25$\pp, three flagships converging within $1$\pp), and that most wrong outputs are \emph{rendered diagrams with wrong geometry}, not broken code --- fluent, confident, and false, the exact failure mode that motivates reading the model's internals.
MMLU-Pro, MATH, and GPQA-Diamond complete the domain grid with programmatic grading; ceiling-level benchmarks are dropped, since metacognition is failure-hungry.

\section{Knowing versus saying}
\label{sec:knowing}

\paragraph{Protocol.}
Four open models spanning architectures (Qwen3.6-27B, hybrid Mamba--attention; GLM-4.7-Flash, MoE; Mistral-Small-24B, dense; Gemma-4-26B-A4B, MoE/VLM-derived) $\times$ the four domains, $\approx$750 records per cell.
Three turns per problem: prospective confidence, attempt, retrospective confidence; grading is external and never shown, so post-attempt revisions are \emph{blind} self-assessment.
Four readouts of the same latent quantity on identical records: stated confidence (0--100); P(True) and answer log-probability (output distribution); and a linear \emph{probe} (logistic regression on the residual stream at the confidence decision token, grouped out-of-fold, reported at a fixed mid--late layer band, not best-of-layer).
Controls: per-fold layer-0 AUROC is exactly 0.500 in every cell (the read site carries no lexical signal); a surface-features baseline adds $+0.22$ mean incremental AUROC for the probe in 15/16 cells; a within-question control fixes difficulty; a public deviations log records four bugs caught by planned controls, fixed, and re-run.

\paragraph{Knowing exceeds saying, most where errors look clean.}
The probe beats stated confidence in \textbf{15 of 16} cells (\cref{tab:knowing}, left).
The gap is widest on MATH: stated confidence there is near-useless (0.57) while the internal signal is the strongest of any domain (0.76--0.90).
Verbalized confidence appears to lean on surface fluency, which multi-step computation defeats; the internal state encodes the computation itself.
Post-attempt internal readouts also exceed pre-attempt ones --- doing the work sharpens the internal signal even where it degrades the stated one.

\paragraph{The signal tracks the attempt, not the question.}
Within a single question, across its five sampled attempts, only attempt-level information can discriminate --- and there the model's own P(True) collapses toward chance while the probe holds (\cref{tab:knowing}, right): P(True) largely re-derives \emph{which questions are hard}; the probe tracks \emph{whether this attempt succeeded}.
Two decompositions sharpen this.
Residualizing post-attempt probe scores on pre-attempt scores leaves substantial AUROC (0.59--0.87), so the post signal is attempt-specific rather than re-expressed difficulty; and the post-attempt direction transfers only weakly to the pre-attempt site, so \emph{did I succeed?} and \emph{is this hard?} are largely distinct directions.
Blind post-attempt confidence drops are also larger on failures than successes: a genuine, under-expressed self-monitoring process.

\paragraph{One correctness direction, largely domain-general.}
Probes trained in one domain and evaluated in another retain $\approx$$87$--$90\%$ of in-domain AUROC (e.g.\ Qwen3.6 off-diagonal 0.71 vs.\ diagonal 0.81), occasionally losslessly within QA domains (Qwen3.6 MMLU-Pro$\to$MATH 0.86 vs.\ 0.91 in-domain).
Geometry is partially special: on Gemma-4, geometry-trained probes \emph{anti-transfer} to QA (0.31--0.39) while QA-trained probes work on geometry ($\approx$0.70) --- probes trained on the generative task can latch onto task-specific features, while QA-trained probes find the general direction.
A shared correctness direction is what a deployable readout needs, and what a workspace account (below) predicts.

\paragraph{The signal is causally used.}
Steering at the confidence token (after the answer, which is unchanged by construction), we amplify the model's own projection onto its correctness direction, with no labels at intervention time.
On Mistral $\times$ MATH, as gain increases ($1\to2\to4$), stated confidence on \emph{failed} answers falls $80 \to 65 \to 43$ while correct answers hold near 90; calibration error halves ($0.31 \to 0.13$); a matched random direction is flat; dampening \emph{increases} overconfidence (a bidirectional dose--response).
Replication is architecture-dependent: Gemma-4 shows the right specificity but is underpowered; GLM is null at tested gains, its stated confidence saturating near 100 precisely where the probe most exceeds P(True).
We read this spectrum as the finding: \emph{how much internal knowledge reaches speech varies by model}.

\paragraph{Toward mechanism: does the knowledge enter the workspace?}
Why does a signal this strong fail to reach speech in some architectures?
The global-workspace account \citep{anthropic2026workspace} offers a testable answer: information becomes reportable only when it enters a verbalizable subspace, and its unsupervised Jacobian lens found ``bug detection before identification'' --- knowing-without-saying --- in code models.
Our prediction: on MATH failures the correctness signal exists (the probe reads it) but does not enter the workspace, hence cannot be said; GLM's decoupled speech channel should show the largest exclusion.
The lens also supplies an unsupervised readout of our saved activations (retiring probe-overfitting critiques) and a label-free steering direction.
This arm is in progress and is, we argue, where the research frontier sits: not \emph{whether} models know, but \emph{what gates the knowing from the saying}.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{For reliable mathematical agents.}
Where a domain admits a verifier, use it: symbolic checks are exact, cheap, and conservative in a known direction.
Where it does not, the internal signal is the best attempt-specific readout we found: abstaining on the probe's lowest-scoring outputs raises accuracy far more than abstaining on low stated confidence (on the cells where verbalized abstention fails outright, internal abstention still works), and the steering result suggests stated confidence itself can be made honest by amplification rather than retraining.
Domain-generality means one direction may serve many tasks.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Linear probes at a single token undercount nonlinear or multi-token signal.
On MATH an early-layer channel adjacent to the answer text may contribute lexical signal that the within-question control cannot fully exclude; the steering test is the discriminator, and it is why we weight the causal arm heavily.
The causal arm is one clean model plus one underpowered and one null replication; two MATH cells run at reduced $n$; the workspace arm has not yet produced results.
Whether the knowing--saying gap is an artifact of preference tuning (testable with a base-model arm) is open.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Anchored by a compiler-graded geometry testbed, we showed that open language models carry a linear, largely domain-general internal signal of their own correctness that exceeds their stated confidence, tracks the individual attempt, and is causally used to set what they say --- to a degree that varies by architecture.
Models substantially \emph{know} when their mathematics is wrong.
The open problem is the gate between knowing and saying, and we have a concrete, falsifiable test of it in flight.
