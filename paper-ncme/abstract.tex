\begin{abstract}
Language models draw geometry diagrams that look right but are wrong. A right
angle comes out at $88^\circ$, and nothing in the picture says so. We have the
model describe the construction instead of picking coordinates. A geometry
engine then computes every point and checks each stated requirement. Because
the item's wording and its checklist come from the same template, one item
model yields the item and its answer key together. On 600 benchmark prompts,
structured generation raised pass rates for every model that could run it. Two
human graders showed the checker is lenient in one direction: it passed 36 of
200 diagrams they failed and failed none they passed. The automatic score is
fit for benchmarking, not yet for high-stakes scoring.
\end{abstract}
