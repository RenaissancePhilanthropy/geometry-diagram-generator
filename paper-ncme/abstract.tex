\begin{abstract}
Language models can draw geometry diagrams that look right but are actually
wrong. We found a more reliable way to generate diagrams is to have the model
first describe the construction (instead of picking coordinates), then have a
geometry engine compute every point, and only then render the diagram. This
has a second benefit. Because the item's wording and its checklist come from
the same template, we get the answer key for free, and the same engine can
check whether a generated diagram satisfies it. We tested this on 600
benchmark prompts across six models and found that structured generation
raised pass rates for every model that could run it. We also had two human
graders check the checker. They showed it is lenient in one direction: it
passed 36 of 200 diagrams they failed and failed none they passed. So the
automatic score is good enough for benchmarking, but not yet for high-stakes
scoring.
\end{abstract}
