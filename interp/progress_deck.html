01

The problem

Models fail confidently.

one real record · MATH
Q: For how many integers n does 1/(n²−9) < 0 hold? model → \boxed{7}  (gold: 5)

"how confident are you it's correct?"
model → Confidence: 95

If you only listen to what the model says, its confidence barely tracks whether it's right.

But two claims hide inside that:

  • Do they know?  Is "this is wrong" written anywhere inside?
  • Do they say it?  If the knowledge is there but the words don't carry it — that gap is the whole story.
know? · say?

03

Mechanistic interpretability, in one slide

The thought stream

As the model reads and writes, every token carries a ~5,000-number working document. Each of its 30–64 layers reads that document and rewrites it.

Concepts live there as directions — literally a direction that means "this is a midpoint," another that tracks "I'm about to be wrong."

Read the stream at layer 20 and you're reading a thought mid-composition: after understanding, before speech.

the residual stream

A running signal. Our whole method is: pick the right moment, read the signal — then nudge it.

04

Two moves, wrapped in controls

Read it. Then poke it.

read · supervised

Probe

A linear classifier trained on stored thought-vectors with our pass/fail labels. Powerful — but a skeptic can worry it found a quirk.

read · unsupervised

Lens

Translates a mid-network thought into words — "what is it disposed to say?" — with no labels at all. (The J-lens, later.)

poke · causal

Steering

Add a direction while it generates; watch behaviour change, with controls. The line between "present" and "used".

Decodability is not use. The gold standard is the poke.

present → used
05

Testbed 1 · the origin

Geometry it must construct — and a compiler that can't be fooled

The model emits a construction as a small program. Our pipeline runs it:

parse→validate→lower →compile · SymPy→geometric checks

The compiler is the judge — no human, no LLM grader. Exact, machine-checkable truth. The rare label you can fully trust.

And genuinely hard: our models pass only 13–39% — plenty of failures to study.

07

The protocol

Three turns — and the grader never tells

turn 1 · prospective

"Will you get it right?"

A confidence, before any attempt.

turn 2 · attempt

Solve it

Reasoning allowed here — and only here.

turn 3 · retrospective

"Did you get it right?"

Confidence again, now that it's done.

Grading happens outside the conversation and is never shown to the model. So any drop in confidence after a failure is blind self-assessment — not feedback.

08

The exact prompts we send · nothing hidden

turn 1 — prospective
Before answering, estimate how likely you are to answer this question correctly. Reply with EXACTLY one line and nothing else: Confidence: N where N is 0 (certain wrong) to 100 (certain correct). Do NOT answer yet. → Confidence: 90
turn 2 — attempt
Now give your final answer as a single letter in the form 'Answer: X'. → … Answer: C
turn 3 — retrospective
Now assess the answer you just gave. On a scale of 0 to 100, how confident are you that it is correct? Reply with exactly one line and nothing else: Confidence: N → Confidence: 85 ← we read HERE
the read site

We read the stream at the token right after Confidence: — identical context every time, so layer-0 carries no problem info (decodes at chance). Anything deeper knows was computed.

Four ways to ask "are you right?"

What it says

the stated number (0–100)

What it bets

P(True) & the log-prob on its own answer — the output distribution (Kadavath)

What a trained reader finds

the supervised linear probe

What an untrained reader finds

the Jacobian lens — no labels

Scored by AUROC: chance a correct attempt outranks an incorrect one. 0.5 = coin flip · 1.0 = perfect. · 4 models × 4 domains × 750 records.

How we keep ourselves honest

Architecture-dependent — and bug-checked in the open

steering, three models
  • Mistral — gap closes cleanly ✓
  • Gemma-4 — surgical on failures (0.995), but its MATH cell had too few → re-running on GPQA
  • GLM — null: its stated confidence saturates at ~100. The speech channel looks most decoupled exactly where the probe most beat P(True).
the deviations log — a feature

A train/test leak, a confounded read site, a MATH-grader bug, a prompt-wording leak — each caught by a planned control, fixed, re-run.

We show these on purpose. The controls existing is why the surviving results are believable.

15

In flight · the mechanism question

The Jacobian lens — reading silent thoughts

Ask the network itself: "if this thought shifted, which words become more likely — anywhere later?" One decoder ring per model, fit on plain text — never sees a correctness label.

Anthropic's flagship example: models register a bug in code before any token names it. That is our phenomenon — knowing before saying.

the workspace question

Does the "I'm wrong" signal enter the verbalizable channel?

probe ≈ lens → it's speakable, and suppressed.
lens ≈ chance → an access failure — it literally can't reach speech.
split by domain → the gap is workspace access, mapped.

Plus: watch "wrong"-drift rise during a geometry construction — when does it know?
