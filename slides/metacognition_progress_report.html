Motivation · the program in one slide

Models fail confidently — a trustworthy "I'm not sure" is a free safety margin

the phenomenon

Models regularly produce a wrong answer and state "Confidence: 95." Nothing in the words separates a right answer from a wrong one — so the wrong one ships.

the question, split

"Doesn't know it's wrong" and "won't say it's wrong" are different claims. Does the knowledge exist inside (read it)? Can we use it (steer it)? The distance between knowing and saying is the study.

Inspiration: psychology's metacognition split — prospective ("will I get this right?") vs retrospective ("did I?"). Our protocol measures both, and reads the stream while it happens.

Left card first — the phenomenon: "Models fail confidently. In our own data they produce a wrong answer and then state 'Confidence: 95' — and nothing in the words separates that from a right answer at 95. So the wrong one ships; nobody double-checks a 95." Right card — the move that makes it research: "'Doesn't know it's wrong' and 'won't say it's wrong' are different claims about different mechanisms. We split them: does the knowledge exist inside — can we read it — and can we do anything with it. The distance between knowing and saying is the whole study."

Why anyone should care, said plainly: a calibrated "I'm not sure" is a free safety margin — a model that knows it's failing can abstain, retry, or escalate to a human. Today that margin exists unused, if the signal is real. That's the bet the program makes.

[→] The inspiration fragment: "We borrowed the framing from psychology — metacognition, with its prospective/retrospective split: 'will I get this right?' before the attempt versus 'did I?' after. Our protocol measures both. The difference from the behavioral-calibration literature — which measures only what models say — is that we also read the residual stream while it happens. That's the mech-interp part, and it's what makes the knowing side measurable at all." ~1 minute total.

how each bar is computed
AUROC = Pr(score✓ > score✗) + ½·Pr(tie)
  1. Grey: score = the confidence number the model stated in turn 3 — the post-task one, after it answered.

Set up the objection with full force — it deserves it: "here's the best objection in the room: 'your probe just learned which questions are hard. Difficulty is decodable too, and that isn't self-monitoring.' Completely fair — and the five repeats per question exist precisely to kill it."

The design: "keep only mixed-outcome questions — where the same model, on the same question, sometimes passed and sometimes failed across its five attempts. E5 · steering causal on Mistral · replicating

Gauge, not wheel: it moves what the model says — not what it gets right

why we ran itDecodable ≠ used. Everything so far is correlation; the causal bar is dose-response + flat random control + sign flip. Design choice that keeps it clean: amplify the model's own signal (gain 1 = exact no-op), inject during the assessment turn only — the answer is already on the page.

What ran: sweep the gain over the model's own signal during turn 3, on Mistral × MATH; track mean stated confidence separately for wrong answers, correct answers, and a matched random direction. Graph next slide.

The same direction cannot make it right: accuracy steering shows no lift (0.38 vs 0.41 baseline, GLM×GPQA — preliminary). Replication is architecture-shaped: GLM null (verbalization saturates ~100), Gemma MATH underpowered.

Next: Gemma × GPQA steering rerun queued · label-free lens-direction steering proposed

The why-box: "everything so far is correlation — decodable is not used. The causal bar: dose-response, flat random control, sign flip. Two design choices keep the experiment clean: we don't inject a fixed vector, we amplify the model's own signal — measure this record's projection on the direction and scale it by a gain, so gain 1 is an exact no-op and no label is used at intervention time. And we inject during the assessment turn only — the answer is already on the page, so the nudge can move the self-report but structurally cannot change the answer."

Graph · E5 evidence causal on Mistral

Turning up the doubt signal lowers false confidence

Mistral × MATH · x-axis = how far we turn the signal up (1× = no change) · dashed grey = a random signal of the same strength.
how we steer
  1. Find the direction. One direction in the model's working memory tracks "am I right?" — we get it by comparing its state on answers it got right vs. wrong.
  2. Turn it up, mid-answer. As the model writes its confidence, we dial its own reading along that direction up or down — a volume knob on a signal it already has, not a new instruction. At 1× nothing changes.
  3. Sweep and watch. Slide the knob (the x-axis) and plot the confidence it then states: red = wrong answers, green = correct, dashed = a random direction.

Why it's causal: the effect grows the more we turn the knob up, a random direction does nothing, and turning it down makes the model more overconfident.

Exact knob: h → h + (g−1)·(û·h − μ)·û

Confidence runs 0–100. As we turn up the model's own doubt, its confidence on wrong answers falls from 80 to 43 — it stops insisting it's right. On correct answers it barely moves (97 to 89): only false confidence deflates.

The formula beside the chart is the entire intervention — walk it: û is the correctness direction from the class means; û·h is how much of it this very record already computed; we scale that component by the gain and add nothing else. Gain one is the identity — the cleanest baseline an intervention can have — and no label is used at intervention time. Injection happens during the assessment turn only, so the answer is already on the page and cannot change.

[→] Then the curves: on wrong answers, stated confidence slides 80 → 65 → 43 as the gain goes 1 → 2 → 4 — clean dose-response. Correct answers barely move (97 → 96 → 89): specific to failures, not generic degradation. Expected calibration error — the mean gap between claimed confidence and realized accuracy — halves, 0.31 to 0.13. The random direction is flat, and dampening makes the model more overconfident — the sign flips. All three pre-registered controls pass. ~90 seconds.
